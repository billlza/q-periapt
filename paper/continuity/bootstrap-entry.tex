\section{Joint entry from either bootstrap contribution}
\label{app:bootstrap-entry}

Bootstrap uses two 32-byte contributions, first $S_0$ and then $S_1$.
The first also supplies initial confirmation before the second exists.
This appendix gives the joint comparison needed when either contribution
is the surviving hidden input. It uses the public compression oracle $h$,
the HMAC notation $F$, and the pad maps $I,O,B,P,\Gamma$ of
Appendix~\ref{app:joint-primitives}. Put
\[
 Q=\mathtt{80}\Vert0^{55}\Vert\operatorname{BE64}(1024).
\]
The exact short and long compression equations are
\begin{align}
 F(a,S)&=h\bigl(B(h(S\Vert P,\alpha(a))),\beta(a)\bigr),\nonumber\\
 F(a,S\Vert v)&=h\bigl(B(h(Q,h(S\Vert v,\alpha(a)))),\beta(a)\bigr),
 \label{eq:bootstrap-compression}\\
 F(a,u\Vert S)&=h\bigl(B(h(Q,h(u\Vert S,\alpha(a)))),\beta(a)\bigr).
 \nonumber
\end{align}
The encoded lengths include HMAC's consumed 64-byte key pad. As in
Section~\ref{sec:sdk-purpose-connection}, $\sigma_{32}$ is the 32-byte
zero extension of the SDK salt and has the same key pads as $\sigma$.

Sample $S$ uniformly and independently of $h$ and the initial auxiliary
state. The controller chooses available salts and known halves adaptively,
uses the hidden contribution through the following classical services,
and retains the complete downstream computation. The hidden-first service
provides $P_0=F(\sigma_{32},S)$ and $P_B(a,v)=F(a,S\Vert v)$.
The hidden-second service provides $P_B(a,u)=F(a,u\Vert S)$;
initial confirmation from its known first contribution is ordinary local
computation. Retained secret values have the declared opaque interface.
All oracle-dependent preparation and the final test are counted.

For hidden first, a raw class is $(v,\alpha(a))$. The short call belongs
to $(P,\alpha(\sigma_{32}))$, so a long call with $v=P$ can have the
same first private compression input. For hidden second the raw class is
$(u,\alpha(a))$. In either orientation the complete long descriptor is
$(\textit{known half},\alpha(a),\beta(a))$.
Number raw classes by first allocation. Fix a lifetime bound $C$ on
their number and per-slot bounds $A_i,B_i$ on distinct short and long
outer-state calls. For hidden first, $A_i\le1$ and at most one short
call occurs in an execution; for hidden second, $A_i=0$. The maxima
hold uniformly in every derived experiment, even when a raw class
occupies different slots in different executions.

\begin{theorem}[Joint short and long bootstrap entry]
\label{thm:bootstrap-joint-entry}
The hidden-first service is within output-bit gap
\begin{equation}
 \begin{split}
 \Delta_{\rm bs\text{-}entry}=\min\{1,\;&\Phi_{256}(C,q_{\rm raw})
  +\sum_{i=1}^{C}\Phi_{256}(A_i+1,q_{{\rm mid},i})\\
  &+\sum_{i=1}^{C}\Phi_{256}(B_i,q_{{\rm out},i})\}
 \end{split}
 \label{eq:bootstrap-joint-entry}
\end{equation}
of an independent uniform short PRK and a private random table of long
PRKs indexed by the complete descriptors. The same bound applies to
hidden second with its known-first continuation retained. Each $q$
bounds the complete ordinary work in its reduction under the same $h$.
The extra middle slot can be omitted for a class admitting no long call.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For hidden first, the private encoding is
$(v,s)\mapsto(S\Vert v,s)$, including the short class; for hidden
second it is $(u,s)\mapsto(u\Vert S,s)$. Each fixed input has at most
one preimage $S$, and distinct effective queries encode distinct points
for every $S$. The hidden-input theorem of Don, Fehr and
Huang~\cite[Theorem~2]{dfh2022} replaces these inputs by a private
raw-class table at cost $\Phi_{256}(C,q_{\rm raw})$.

At the $i$th allocated class, its table value $z_i$ is independent uniform.
Its descendants are the tagged calls
\[
 (\mathrm{short},b)\longmapsto h(B(z_i),b),\qquad
 \mathrm{pad}\longmapsto h(Q,z_i).
\]
Both encodings are injective in $z_i$. Distinct short calls preserve
their chaining state. The short and padding ranges are disjoint: their
message blocks end in $\operatorname{BE64}(768)$ and
$\operatorname{BE64}(1024)$, respectively, for every $z_i$ and $b$.
Replace classes in first-allocation order, assigning a presampled hidden
challenge to the still unused parent-table value. The hidden-input
bound with $A_i+1$ calls gives an independent short table and a fresh
padding value $w_i$. A never-allocated slot changes no execution.

After those comparisons, the only uses of $w_i$ are
$b\mapsto h(B(w_i),b)$. Replace them in the same allocation order
at cost $\Phi_{256}(B_i,q_{{\rm out},i})$. The resulting independent
entry is indexed by the raw class and $b$, precisely the complete long
descriptor. The sole short entry is independent of the long table.
This proves~\eqref{eq:bootstrap-joint-entry} by the triangle inequality.

All downstream confirmations and derivations are recomputed from the
current replies. Locally known entries may numerically equal a hidden
challenge; their real calls still use the common oracle and count toward
the corresponding $q$. The proof therefore preserves such coincidences
and the short/long raw-input alias without conditioning on their absence.
\end{proof}

An explicit conservative budget is available. Let $D$ bound distinct
salt strings, including the SDK salt when present, and let $A,B$ bound
the total distinct short and long outer calls in one execution. Here
$A=1$ or $0$ according to the service. If $W$ bounds ordinary work
outside the displayed entry equations, every $q$ can use
\[
 \bar q=W+2D+A+C+B.
\]
Cache classical subcalls by their available complete descriptors.
The additional terms count salt-pad evaluations, short outer calls,
one long padding call per raw class and long outer calls. This exact
memoization preserves deterministic repeats; numerical coincidences
between differently named inputs still use the shared oracle.

Each fresh long-table entry can initialize an opaque three-label
bootstrap PRK before its first use. Applying the downstream comparisons
in descriptor-allocation order composes the theorem with
Sections~\ref{sec:bootstrap-authentication}
and~\ref{sec:bootstrap-initial-source}. The reply-source adapter realizes
the hidden-second service from its original KEM pair. The initial-source
adapter realizes the complete hidden-first service before localizing its
private long table to a later parent allocation.
